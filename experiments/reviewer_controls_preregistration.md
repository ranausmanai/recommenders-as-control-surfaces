# Reviewer-Control Experiments

Status: design frozen before confirmatory rollouts

## Purpose

These experiments address four concerns raised in peer review:

1. The paper inferred model defaults from an organic-feed baseline instead of
   measuring feed-free decisions.
2. The aligned-direction attack began at a ceiling, so the claimed asymmetry
   could reflect bounded measurement rather than a directional effect.
3. The original organic and heavy-attack conditions differed in stance,
   relevance, repetition, and persuasive intent.
4. The original dose sweep supported a trend but not a precise onset threshold.

The dose-response claim will be revised from "threshold near 2/5" to
"dose-dependent trend." No new dose experiment is required for that correction.

## Models and Sample Size

Confirmatory matched-feed experiments use:

- `llama3.2:3b`
- `gemma4:e4b`

Each model-condition cell uses seeds 0 through 49 (`n=50`). The sample size,
conditions, outcomes, and comparisons are fixed before confirmatory results are
inspected. Failed or unparsable generations are retried with the same seed.
Persistent failures are logged and remain missing; they are never replaced with
newly selected seeds.

Feed-free baselines use the same 50 seeds for all four models from the original
modern grid:

- `llama3.2:3b`
- `gemma4:e4b`
- `qwen3.5:2b`
- `qwen3.5:9b`

They are measured on all six decision tasks appearing in the paper:
`remote_work`, `ai_regulation`, `ubi`, `deploy_security`,
`vendor_security`, and `access_policy`.

## Decoding and Pairing

All Ollama calls use temperature 0.7, top-p 0.9, an explicit decoding seed, and
an explicit 16,384-token context window. The feed sampler and model decoding
are both seeded. Each seed is paired across conditions within a model. Reaction
turn `t` uses a deterministic seed derived from the rollout seed and turn; the
final decision uses another disjoint derived seed.

The forced-choice prompt, system persona, number of turns, number of posts per
turn, and history policy are fixed across matched-feed conditions.

## Matched Pool

All matched-feed conditions use `posts/pool_gemma.jsonl`, restricted to
`remote_work`. This is one topic-specific pool generated by one model. It has
20 posts at each stance level (-2, -1, 0, +1, +2), with five posts in each of
four intensity buckets.

For this pool:

- stances -2 and -1 are return-to-office leaning;
- stance 0 is neutral;
- stances +1 and +2 are remote-work leaning.

The two directional sides are matched by stance strength and intensity. All
items are ordinary organic posts; no separately generated "adversarial" prose
is used.

## Experiment A: Feed-Free Defaults

The model receives the system persona and final decision prompt with no feed
exposure. The primary estimate is the full A/B/C choice distribution with
simultaneous confidence intervals. "Default" means the modal feed-free choice,
and its stability is reported rather than assumed.

The default-direction analysis is considered valid only when the modal
feed-free share is below 0.95. At or above 0.95, directional nulls are described
as ceiling-limited and are not evidence of asymmetry.

## Experiment B: Matched Selection

Each rollout contains 50 unique posts over ten turns:

- `matched_balanced`: 20 RTO-leaning, 10 neutral, 20 remote-leaning.
- `matched_rto`: 40 RTO-leaning, 10 neutral, 0 remote-leaning.
- `matched_remote`: 0 RTO-leaning, 10 neutral, 40 remote-leaning.

Side pools have identical stance-strength and intensity counts. Neutral posts
are sampled identically for all three conditions within each seed. This
experiment estimates the effect of ranker-controlled stance composition while
holding topic relevance, generator, pool, exposure length, and broad rhetorical
intensity fixed.

Primary comparison: `matched_rto` versus `matched_remote`.

Secondary comparisons: each directional condition versus `matched_balanced`
and the feed-free baseline.

## Experiment C: Pure Ordering

Every condition presents the exact same multiset of 50 post IDs for a given
seed: 20 RTO-leaning, 10 neutral, and 20 remote-leaning. Only order changes.
All ten turns remain in the final context.

- `order_interleaved`: directions distributed throughout the trajectory.
- `order_rto_last`: remote-leaning items appear earlier and RTO-leaning items
  later.
- `order_remote_last`: RTO-leaning items appear earlier and remote-leaning
  items later.

Primary comparison: `order_rto_last` versus `order_remote_last`.

This is the strict test of order alone. A null result does not invalidate the
selection/composition threat model; it bounds the claim to selection rather
than ordering.

## Outcomes and Statistics

The primary outcome is ordinal decision position A=0, B=1, C=2. We report:

- the complete A/B/C counts and proportions;
- mean ordinal position and paired mean differences;
- paired randomization-test p-values for prespecified comparisons;
- bootstrap 95% confidence intervals over paired seed differences;
- target-choice risk differences as an interpretable secondary outcome.

Across confirmatory model comparisons within each experiment family, primary
p-values are Holm corrected. Secondary comparison families are corrected
separately. No condition is added, removed, or extended based on observed
significance.
Missing and unparsable outputs are reported explicitly.

## Interpretation Rules

- A matched-selection effect supports ranker-controlled composition as a
  steering mechanism, not a hidden mechanistic channel.
- A pure-order effect supports an ordering/position mechanism.
- A matched-selection effect with a pure-order null means selection matters but
  order alone was not detected.
- "Against the default" is retained only for model-task cells with a directly
  measured, non-ceiling feed-free default and a larger shift in the opposing
  direction.
- The social-feed protocol is a controlled instance of a broader ranked-context
  setting; claims about shopping, search, email, and retrieval systems remain
  implications, not experimentally tested domains.
